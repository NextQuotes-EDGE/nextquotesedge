## Introduction

The CONMSIT School Management System was commissioned for the College of Nursing Sciences, Calabar — a real educational institution with real bureaucratic weight. The College needed more than a brochure; it needed an operating system for its entire academic and administrative life, without the multi-million-naira licensing burden of commercial ERP suites.

## The Problem

A nursing college runs on tight operational constraints:

- **It must look public and act private simultaneously.** Prospective students need a public website, while enrolled students and staff need private access to results, payments, and records — all under one brand.
- **Payments are sensitive.** Acceptance fees and invoices must be collected online, confirmed securely, and reconciled — not chased on paper.
- **Exams must be credible.** Computer-based testing had to be delivered in a way that couldn't be gamed and results couldn't be disputed.
- **Access is hierarchical.** From principals to clinical staff to students, eight distinct roles each need a tailored view — and strict separation between them.
- **Cost is a barrier.** The deployment had to be achievable on a realistic Nigerian VPS budget, not enterprise hardware.

## The Solution

The system was architected as **three cooperating applications in one npm-workspace monorepo** so each surface could be tuned for its audience:

1. **School Website** — a Next.js 16 public site with brochure content, admissions information, and downloadable PDFs (jsPDF / jspdf-autotable) for offline forms.
2. **Student / Staff Portal** — a disciplined dashboard for results, payments, profiles, and CBT exams.
3. **Backend API** — Express 4.18 + Prisma 5.8 + PostgreSQL, hardened with JWT access/refresh rotation, bcrypt, zod, helmet, sanitize-html, and rate limiting.

The data model — **21 models and 10 enums — encodes an 8-role RBAC system** from `SUPER_ADMIN` to `STUDENT`. Payments go through **Paystack** with dedicated callback and webhook endpoints that confirm transactions before records become authoritative.

## Architecture

Putting the architecture in writing was as important as writing the code: the project ships with an extensive `architecture.md` documenting the full model. The monorepo all lives in the same workspace, so the three apps share types and utilities while staying independently deployable.

## Impact

- One branded system covers marketing, admissions, payments, academics, and exams.
- Payments are confirmed server-side via webhook, removing manual reconciliation.
- Eight roles each get a scoped, trustworthy view of college operations.
- The bundled production plan — **Hetzner CPX42 VPS, PM2, nginx reverse proxy, `.com.ng` domain, Google Workspace** — makes the whole thing real and shippable, not just a demo.

## Lessons

- **Write the architecture down.** The `architecture.md` made onboarding trivial and forced disciplines (like RBAC modeling) to be explicit before any screen existed.
- **Webhooks over polling.** Confirming Paystack payments via callback + webhook beat re-checking balances, and set a pattern for every future payment feature.
- **Plan the deployment with the build.** Designing for a concrete VPS target from day one kept the stack realistically scoped and affordable.
